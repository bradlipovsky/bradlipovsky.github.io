<section class="research-section" id="fiber-sensing">
  <div class="research-section__title">
    <p>Research program 01</p>
    <h2>Optical fiber geophysics</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="research-section__body">
    <p>Distributed fiber sensors turn ordinary optical cable into a continuous array of seismic, acoustic, strain, and temperature measurements. We develop the technology and the physical interpretation required to use those measurements as observatories.</p>
    <p>The work spans boreholes, glaciers, volcanoes, cities, and the seafloor. It also tackles the infrastructure challenge: how to move, store, compress, and interpret measurements from instruments that can generate terabytes per day.</p>
    <div class="research-topics">
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Long-range and multi-span sensing</strong><span>Observations across optical repeaters and large submarine cable systems.</span></div>
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Glacier and borehole sensing</strong><span>Dense measurements of temperature, deformation, meltwater, and basal processes.</span></div>
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Wavefield reconstruction</strong><span>Compression, separation, calibration, and edge computation for massive datasets.</span></div>
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Research translation</strong><span>Moving fiber sensing into sustained monitoring for infrastructure and hazards.</span></div>
    </div>
    <p><a class="text-link" href="https://fiberlab.uw.edu">Visit the UW Fiber Lab</a></p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section" id="cryosphere">
  <div class="research-section__title">
    <p>Research program 02</p>
    <h2>Ice fracture and glacier dynamics</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="research-section__body">
    <p>Ice sheets and glaciers change through coupled processes at their beds, surfaces, margins, and ocean boundaries. We combine fracture mechanics, glacier flow models, seismology, and remote sensing to understand those connections.</p>
    <p>The work links fundamental mechanics to high-consequence questions: how ice shelves lose stability, how glaciers slide, how water moves beneath ice, and how ice–ocean interactions alter glacier geometry and sea-level projections.</p>
    <div class="research-topics">
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Ice-shelf rifts and calving</strong><span>Rupture, flexure, shear margins, ocean coupling, tides, and thermal stress.</span></div>
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Glacier basal processes</strong><span>Stick-slip, icequakes, friction, sediment entrainment, and subglacial hydrology.</span></div>
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Ice–ocean interaction</strong><span>Fjord dynamics, frontal processes, floating ice, and water-terminating glaciers.</span></div>
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Old ice and climate archives</strong><span>Fiber-optic borehole observations and models of Antarctic blue-ice dynamics.</span></div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section" id="hazards">
  <div class="research-section__title">
    <p>Research program 03</p>
    <h2>Environmental geophysics and hazards</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="research-section__body">
    <p>Many Earth hazards share common physics: stress accumulates, materials weaken, rupture begins, and waves carry information away. We use those connections to move ideas and tools between earthquakes, volcanoes, landslides, glaciers, oceans, and engineered systems.</p>
    <p>This cross-system approach makes new observations more useful. Signals recorded on a cable can reveal an earthquake source, volcanic activity, ocean waves, marine mammals, groundwater conditions, or the onset of slope failure—provided the mechanics are understood.</p>
    <div class="research-topics">
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Earthquakes and subduction zones</strong><span>Source processes, early warning, offshore seismicity, and dense wavefields.</span></div>
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Volcano and landslide monitoring</strong><span>New observations at Mount Rainier and physics of catastrophic mass movement.</span></div>
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Oceans and marine acoustics</strong><span>Surface gravity waves, ship noise, whale calls, and hydroacoustic signals.</span></div>
      <div class="research-topic"><strong>Geothermal and infrastructure systems</strong><span>Subsurface characterization, induced processes, and continuous monitoring.</span></div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
